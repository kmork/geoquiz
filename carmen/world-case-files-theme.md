# World Case Files Theme

## Core Premise

World Case Files is built around **The Return Atlas**: a group of cultural thieves who steal famous artifacts and walk them backward through their cultural biographies. They claim every object has one true home, but their methods are criminal and their history is too simple. They forge provenance cards, erase inconvenient movement, and turn complex cultural lives into clean ideological routes.

ACME is not simply defending museums. The player is defending evidence, context, and cultural truth. Each case asks the player to recover the artifact by learning where it came from, how it moved, who interpreted it, where it influenced culture, and where the thief replaced history with a false certainty.

## Design Goals

- Combine cultural learning with geo-learning.
- Make every travel stop a meaningful chapter in the artifact's life.
- Avoid trivia-only clues; clues should explain why a city, site, museum, archive, or route matters.
- Keep the thief's claim plausible but incomplete.
- Let the player learn that origin, ownership, influence, display, discovery, and cultural identity are related but not identical.

## Case-Worthy Artifact Rule

A World Case artifact should support at least five meaningful stops without padding. Strong candidates usually satisfy most of these checks:

- It was made, used, discovered, or culturally rooted in one place but displayed elsewhere.
- It passed through empire, war, excavation, sale, diplomacy, colonial collection, trade, scholarship, or restoration.
- It has modern debate, influence, identity value, or contested interpretation.
- It connects to named cities, museums, archives, sites, routes, or cultural institutions.
- It teaches geography through cultural movement rather than only through national facts.

Shorter routes are acceptable for prototypes, but they should be flagged as underdeveloped until each stop has a clear cultural reason and the case reaches a richer biography shape.

## Artifact Biography Route Model

Use this model when authoring or expanding a case:

1. **Display**: where the object is now held, shown, or stolen from.
2. **Transfer**: how it entered a collection, archive, market, empire, or museum system.
3. **Archive**: where scholars, conservators, translators, or cataloguers changed how people understand it.
4. **Movement**: a route through trade, war, excavation, diplomacy, collecting, or artistic influence.
5. **Origin**: where it was made, used, discovered, sacred, commissioned, or culturally rooted.
6. **Forgery**: where the thief stages a false return or rewrites the record to make one claim look final.

Not every case needs these exact six stages, but every route stop should declare its stage, learning goal, thief claim, and truth complication.

## Recommended Artifact Families

### Provenance And Restitution

- **Benin Bronzes**: Edo court art, Benin City, Portuguese contact, 1897 British expedition, colonial dispersal, modern restitution.
- **Parthenon Sculptures**: Acropolis origin, Ottoman-era removal, British Museum display, Acropolis Museum claim, modern restitution debate.
- **Bust of Nefertiti**: Amarna workshop, 1912 excavation, Berlin display, beauty icon, Egyptian return claims.

### Knowledge And Decipherment

- **Rosetta Stone**: Ptolemaic decree, Rashid/Rosetta discovery, multilingual scripts, Cairo/Alexandria context, Paris/London scholarship, Egyptology.
- **Codex Mendoza**: Mexica tribute and history, Spanish colonial administration, manuscript travel, European archives.
- **Cyrus Cylinder**: Babylonian context, Achaemenid rule, excavation, British Museum display, modern political interpretation.

### Influence And World Travel

- **The Great Wave off Kanagawa**: Hokusai, Edo print culture, Dutch/Japanese collecting, Japonisme, Western modern art, American collections.
- **Mona Lisa**: Leonardo, Renaissance Italy, artist mobility, French royal collecting, Louvre display, conservation and copy history.
- **Pergamon Altar or Ishtar Gate**: ancient city, archaeological excavation, imperial museum reconstruction, source-site identity.

## First Serious Arc Recommendation

Use a mixed iconic set so the mode does not collapse into one repeated restitution pattern:

1. **Mona Lisa**: art fame and Renaissance biography.
2. **Rosetta Stone**: language, Egyptology, and imperial transfer.
3. **Benin Bronzes**: colonial looting, royal court culture, and restitution.
4. **Bust of Nefertiti**: excavation, modern icon-making, and contested display.
5. **The Great Wave off Kanagawa**: print culture and global artistic influence.

## Gameplay Translation

- The evidence board should show the active biography stage, not only the current country.
- Cultural-place map pins should feel like clue locations, not decorative markers.
- Candidate-map coloring should respond to cultural evidence as well as geographic clues.
- The final proof should expose both the correct destination and the thief's oversimplified claim.
- Authoring checks should warn when a case is too short, lacks stage metadata, repeats stages without reason, or does not reveal the false simplification.
